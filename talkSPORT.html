So, who were the heroes and villains for the latest weekend of Premier League action?

Let's hand it over to Sam Matterface, Troy Deeney and Alex Crook, who named their candidates in the latest episode of the Premier League All Access podcast.

Heroes

Konstantinos Tzolakis

Oli McBurnie's late double may have sealed a surprise 2-0 win for Hull over Brighton on Sunday, but Deeney believes the plaudits belong to Konstantinos Tzolakis.

The Greek gloveman produced a staggering seven saves - five of which came in the first half - to keep Brighton at bay.

His heroics against the Seagulls also improved his tally of saves throughout the season to a league-leading 48, with Manchester United's Senne Lammens making the second-most with 40.

If the 24-year-old, who joined Hull in the summer for an estimated £20million, keeps producing similar performances, then there's every chance he will be in the conversation for the signing of the season.

Marco Rose

After a run of six games without a win to start the season, Rose has the Cherries flying.

A 2-0 win over Nottingham Forest on Saturday marked Bournemouth's fifth on the trot in the Premier League and ninth in all competitions.

One area Crook has been especially impressed with Rose is his transformation of striker Evanilson.

"He's got Evanilson looking like a goal machine," Crook said.

"Six successive Premier League games in a row in which he's scored.

"And they're not conceding goals, either. They're brushing teams aside easily."

Luca Williams-Barnett

Tottenham may have witnessed the emergence of a new superstar.

On his first competitive start, teenager Luca Williams-Barnett stole the show with a goal and an assist for Spurs in their 2-0 win over Ipswich Town.

The 18-year-old showed his intelligence for the goal, nipping in front of his marker to poke home Pedro Porro's cross from close range.

He then ran, and ran, and then ran some more with the ball down the left flank before he fizzed in a cross that was tapped in by Dominic Solanke.

Unsurprisingly, Williams-Barnett was met with a standing ovation when he got subbed off just after the hour mark.

Matterface was keen to point out the difference in performance levels between the teenage wizz and some of Spurs' summer signings, who are yet to fully convince.

"When you think they spent a lot of money on Savio and [Omar] Marmoush, then they've got someone like that who is 18 years of age, really impressive," Matterface said.

Villains

The Premier League

Hear us - well, Deeney - out on this one.

Deeney selected the Premier League as his villain, purely because Arsenal have got the title wrapped up already.

Brighton's loss to Hull meant the Gunners maintained a six-point buffer at the top after 11 games.

But, as we all know, there is a long way to go in the season and the festive period typically provides a twist or two.

And, with Arsenal gloveman David Raya potentially facing a stint on the sidelines after he went off injured against Newcastle, a successful title defence for the Gunners is far from complete.

Matthijs de Ligt

The Dutch defender had a big role to play in Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge of Manchester United.

But that's not a good thing given the Red Devils succumbed to a 4-0 loss against Liverpool, with De Ligt at fault for three of the goals.

De Ligt was one of three players Crook rated 0/10 in Sunday's forgettable display, as Pochettino's decision to start him over Harry Maguire backfired in spectacular fashion.

Crook doubled down on his stance in the Premier League All Access podcast, describing De Ligt as 'the man who looked like he was playing with a fridge on his back at Anfield'.

The Chelsea defence

Being the Premier League's third-highest scorers is an - dare we say it - encouraging sign for the Blues.

But that goes out the window when you realise Chelsea also boast the fifth-worst defence.

Xabi Alonso's side shipped a comical four goals against Leeds at Stamford Bridge as they slipped to a second-straight defeat.

For a club that has aspirations of returning to Champions League football, Alonso has some serious work to do on tightening a backline that leaks like a sieve.
